Non-Tariff Barriers Across the Region

The East African Community spans multiple markets, and its customs union promises free movement of goods. In practice, exporters still need to understand each border's requirements.

In practice, trade between member states remains stuck at around 15 percent of their total trade. The reason is non-tariff barriers - surprise levies, permit delays, extra inspections, duplicated paperwork and road user charges.

Tanzania accounts for 11 of 28 outstanding barriers, or 39.3 percent.

A Live Example - Mombasa, August 2026

The Kenya Revenue Authority introduced a new Export Declaration documentation requirement effective 3 August 2026.

Clearing agents reported a logjam of vehicles and transit cargo at the Port of Mombasa, container freight stations and inland container depots. Cargo shipped before the requirement took effect could not be cleared.

Importers and transporters became exposed to storage, demurrage and detention charges. Agents argued the requirement duplicated documentation already generated in the exporting country.

Mombasa handles cargo for Uganda, Burundi, South Sudan, eastern DR Congo and northern Tanzania. A single documentation change therefore affects several countries.

Documentation - The Hidden Cost

Kenya has digitised many procedures through its National Electronic Single Window System. Exporters are nonetheless required to obtain multiple documents before goods move, including certificates of origin, phytosanitary and export health certificates, customs declarations, commercial invoices, transport documents, insurance certificates and product-specific laboratory or regulatory approvals.

Documentation costs for a typical export consignment are estimated at KSh 15,000 to 30,000, excluding freight and taxes.

Each border has its own realities. This is why practical, on-the-ground support matters for exporters entering the region.
